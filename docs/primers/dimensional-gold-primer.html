Contents

01

Three things a list of transactions cannot say

After this: you can state why the existing contract had to be rewritten at all.

The Gold contract you have today hands out one thing: a list of transactions, through list_transactions(). That sounds like everything. It isn't, and here are the three sentences it structurally cannot produce.

You want to askWhy a transaction list can't answer
"What was in my savings account in February?" Nothing happened in February, so there are no rows, so there is no balance. The money was there the whole time and the list is silent about it.
"Did I really spend nothing in June, or do I just not have June?" Both are zero rows. Identical. This is the failure the whole import stack exists to prevent, and a bare list reintroduces it at the last step.
"How much went on Housing?" "Housing" is a group of categories. The list has a category on each row and nowhere for the group to live, so every consumer would hardcode its own grouping.

Each of those could be patched by having the dashboard work it out. That is exactly what the current documents say: analytics derives coverage, analytics sums the balances, analytics knows the groups. Then forecasting arrives and derives it again, slightly differently, and an API arrives and derives it a third way.

The one sentence

Gold should publish the answers, not the raw material the answers are worked out from — because every consumer that works them out separately is a chance for two screens to disagree about your February.

02

Facts, dimensions, grain — the whole of Kimball you need

After this: you can read the phrase "declared grain" and know what it commits you to.

Three words carry the entire design. They are less abstract than they sound.

WordMeansHere
FactA thing that happened, with numbers on it you can add up.A booked transaction. A month-end balance.
DimensionA thing you slice the facts by. It has no numbers to add.Account, category, date.
GrainWhat exactly one row means. Stated in one sentence, before anything else."One booked transaction on one account."

Grain is the one that bites. Once you declare "one row = one booked transaction", you have also declared that a row cannot be half groceries and half homeware — because that would need two rows, and two rows would mean a different grain. That is module 03, and it is the decision the ticket specifically refused to let an agent make by accident.

03

The receipt with two categories on it

After this: you can say what ADR-008 gives up, and what it would cost to change your mind later.

You spend 640 kr at Netto. 510 kr of it is food; 130 kr is a frying pan. Under this model that transaction gets one category, and you pick which. There is no way to split it. That is ADR-008, and it is deliberate.

What splitting would actually require

  • A screen where you sit and divide a transaction up, by hand, transaction by transaction.
  • A rule that the parts always add back to the total — and something sensible to do when they don't.
  • An answer for what happens when the frying pan is returned: which part of the original does the refund reverse?

None of that is hard exactly. It is just a workflow you would have to use forever, to make the Housing total slightly more accurate than it already is. The bank gives one row per purchase and no line items, so nothing in the source knows about the frying pan anyway.

The door it leaves open

If you do want splits later, they arrive as a second fact standing beside the transaction fact — "one category allocation of one transaction" — not as a change to what a transaction row means. The existing rows keep their meaning and their IDs. It costs a contract version bump and a new ADR, and nothing you have already recorded is invalidated.

The rejected middle option is worth knowing about: build the allocation fact now, with exactly one allocation per transaction, so the structure is ready. It was turned down because it adds a type, a join and an invariant that buy you nothing at all until the day splits actually exist.

04

Three dimensions, and none of them remember

After this: you can predict what happens to last year's reports when you rename a category.

Account, Category, Date. That is the whole list. Two things about them matter.

Category has exactly two levels, and always two

groceries sits inside the group food. rent and utilities sit inside housing. You assign the category; you never assign the group. Depth is fixed at two — not a tree you can nest arbitrarily — because a tree of unknown depth needs recursive queries to roll anything up, and two levels already gives you "Housing".

Nothing has a memory (Type 1)

Rename groceries to "Food & household" and every report you have ever run changes with it, back to the beginning. Move it into a different group and last January's Housing total moves too. There is no "it was called X back then".

The distinction that saves this

Deciding that a particular old transaction was actually utilities, not groceries, is not a dimension change. It is a change to that transaction's classification — a fact change — and keeping a history of those is issue #8's problem, not this model's. Type 1 dimensions don't stop you doing it.

Keys you chose, never keys the bank chose

An account is joint-current. A category is groceries. These are names you assign and they never change. They are explicitly never derived from an IBAN or a bank account number — switch banks and your history survives intact.

There is also no calendar table. A date is its own key, and the reporting month is derived from it. Nothing in this release needs to know about holidays or pay periods, so storing a table of them would be furniture.

05

A row for every month, including the quiet ones

After this: you can explain why an absent row means something different from a zero.

The second fact is MonthlyBalanceSnapshot: one row per account per month, with an opening balance, a closing balance, and a coverage label. A month where nothing happened still gets a row. That is the entire point of it.

joint-savings — one transaction in January, one in April, four rows
MonthOpeningClosingCoverageWhat the row says
2026-0150.000,0053.000,00partialFirst month we track — nothing before it to check against.
2026-0253.000,0053.000,00completeNothing happened, and we can prove it.
2026-0353.000,0053.000,00completeSame.
2026-0453.000,0053.012,40complete12,40 kr of interest.

Which months get a row: the managed period

From the month of the account's first booked transaction, through the month it closed — or, while it is open, through the latest month in the published data. Not "today". Using today would mean a rebuild next Tuesday produces different rows from a rebuild today, which makes the output impossible to compare with itself.

So closed_on is a real field on the account now, replacing a vague active/inactive flag. Without it a closed account would keep producing rows forever, every one of them saying no_data.

The rule to remember

No row at all means "outside the period we track this account". A row saying no_data means "we track it, and we don't know". A row saying complete with zero activity means "we track it, and we checked, and nothing happened". Three different statements, three different shapes on the page.

06

Balances add sideways, never downwards

After this: you can spot the wrong sum at a glance.

Amounts add up any way you like. Balances don't — they are what Kimball calls semi-additive, and it is worth ten seconds of your attention because the wrong sum looks entirely plausible on a chart.

SumVerdict
April, across accounts
52.087,50 + 53.012,40		 ✓ 105.099,90 — two different pots of money at the same moment. This is your net worth in April, and it is a real number.
joint-current, Feb + Mar + Apr
37.537,50 + 35.587,50 + 52.087,50		 ✗ 125.212,50 — meaningless. Mostly the same kroner counted three times. There is no question this number answers.

The contract states this as an invariant, so the rule travels with the data instead of living in whoever happens to be writing the dashboard. The same section also forbids adding amounts across currencies — irrelevant today, since everything is DKK, but it means that adding a second currency later cannot quietly corrupt a total that used to be right.

Currency, incidentally, lives on the account, not on each transaction. Every amount and balance is in its account's currency, full stop.

07

The chain check moves into Gold

After this: you can read a balance_check value and say what it proves.

Each transaction carries the bank's stated balance after it. Walk an account in order and each balance should be the previous one plus this amount. Gold now does that walk itself and stamps the result on every transaction.

StampMeans
openingFirst transaction we have for this account. Nothing earlier to check it against, so it is taken on trust and becomes the anchor.
consistentThe arithmetic ties out. This is the ordinary case.
breakIt doesn't tie out. Money moved that we cannot see.
missing_balanceThe source stated no balance on this row. The check bridges over it and carries on — it never invents the missing number.

A break does not cascade

After a break, the chain re-anchors on the broken row's own stated balance and carries on. One unexplained 500 kr does not turn the rest of your year red. In the worked example, transaction 8 breaks and transaction 9 is consistent again:

joint-current, late February into March
seqDateAmountBalanceCheck
72026-02-2525.000,0037.537,50✓ consistent
82026-03-02−450,0036.587,50✗ break — expected 37.087,50, off by 500,00
92026-03-20−1.000,0035.587,50✓ consistent — re-anchored on 36.587,50

Why Gold and not the dashboard

Doing this walk needs two things the dashboard has no business holding: the order of transactions within a single date, which only the import layer knows, and each account's managed period. It also needs doing exactly once — if forecasting and an API each re-derive it, they will eventually disagree.

This is the part of ADR-007 that changes an earlier decision: the accepted documents said analytics derives all this. The meaning of every check is unchanged. Only the place it happens moves.

08

Why February is partial when nothing about February is wrong

After this: you can explain the most counterintuitive rule in the model.

Each account-month carries one of three labels:

LabelMeans
completeEvidence spans the whole month and every link in and out of it checks out. A zero here is a proven zero.
partialThere is data, but there is a gap or a broken link. Don't trust the total.
no_dataNo evidence reaches this month. The system has nothing to say, and the dashboard must never draw that as a zero.

The surprising part

In the worked example, every single February check is consistent. Every row ties out. February is still partial.

The reason is that a link joins two consecutive transactions, and its span covers both their dates. The broken link runs from 25 February to 2 March, so its span includes the end of February. The 500 kr that went missing could have moved on either side of midnight on the 28th — nothing in the data says which. Calling February complete would be claiming to know something you don't.

The same break, seen as coverage
MonthOpeningClosingCoverageWhy
2026-0221.557,5037.537,50partialEvery check consistent — but the March break's link starts here.
2026-0337.037,5035.587,50partialThe break itself. Note the opening is 500,00 below February's closing — that gap is the missing money, visible on the page.
2026-0435.587,5052.087,50completePast the break. 35.587,50 − 8.500,00 + 25.000,00 = 52.087,50.

Worth noticing

March's opening balance is not February's closing balance. They differ by exactly the 500 kr that is unaccounted for. The model doesn't paper over the gap and it doesn't correct it — it puts the discrepancy somewhere you can see it. That is the same instinct as quarantining a bad export rather than importing three quarters of it.

09

A refund stops pretending to be an adjustment

After this: you can check the February expense figure yourself, and see what it used to be.

Your glossary says, in your own words: "A Refund is not an Adjustment." The contract encoded a refund as transaction_type = adjustment with a category attached, because that avoided a contract change at the time. The contract is being rewritten anyway, so the glossary wins: refund is now its own type.

One thing gets simpler immediately — the rule for when a category is required:

TypeCategory
income · expense · refundRequired.
transfer · adjustment · unknownAlways null.

No exceptions, no conditional. Previously adjustment was both, depending.

And Expenses changes — check this one

Under the old wording, household Expenses was the absolute sum of expense transactions, while category spending netted refunds off. So the categories didn't add up to the total. February, worked both ways:

February on joint-current: rent 8.500,00 · groceries 640,00 · a 120,00 grocery refund
MeasureOldNew
Housing (group)8.500,008.500,00
Food (group)520,00520,00
Categories add to9.020,009.020,00
Household Expenses9.140,00—
Household Expenses—9.020,00

The new rule is Expenses = |sum of expenses| − sum of refunds, so 9.140,00 − 120,00 = 9.020,00, and the two rows finally agree. Your savings rate goes up slightly, correctly, because the 120 kr came back.

This reverses a decision you already made

The adjustment encoding was a deliberate choice on issue #4, taken to avoid exactly this kind of contract churn. Overturning it is defensible — but it is the clearest case on this page of an agent changing something a human had settled. It is item one on the check-first list.

10

Two doors, and who is allowed through each

After this: you can say what the dashboard is not allowed to see, and why that matters.

Everything a report needs comes through one small interface:

class GoldRepository(Protocol):
    def accounts(self)          -> Sequence[GoldAccount]: ...
    def categories(self)        -> Sequence[GoldCategory]: ...
    def transactions(self, *, start_date, end_date, account_ids=None) -> ...
    def monthly_balances(self, *, start_month, end_month, account_ids=None) -> ...

Four methods. Ranges are inclusive. Nothing in there names a table, a CSV column, or anything the bank invented. Behind a separate door, for review and audit tooling only: 

class GoldLineageRepository(Protocol):
    def transaction_lineage(self, transaction_ids) -> Sequence[GoldTransactionLineage]: ...

What moved behind that second door

  • silver_transaction_id — the link back to the raw record. The current contract hands this to analytics, which its own agent brief forbids. This fixes that.
  • classification_source and classification_version — whether a category came from a rule or from you, and which rule set said so.

And what was dropped entirely

FieldWhy it went
reporting_monthDerivable from the date. Storing it twice means it can disagree with itself.
created_atThat is publication metadata — when a build ran. Issue #8's business.
counterpartyNothing produces it and no measure uses it. It can come back as a real dimension through issue #7 if merchant reporting ever matters.

The practical effect of the split: if you ever want to know why a transaction is filed under groceries, that question has an answer and a place to ask it — and the dashboard still cannot reach into the raw import to find out.

13

The comment is already out of date in five places

After this: you know where to trust the PR over the comment, and two gaps to close before merging.

The grilling ran before PR #16 merged into this branch. Reconciling the two changed some answers. Where the comment and PR #18 disagree, the PR is current.

Quiet months are complete, not no_dataQ20 reversed

The comment decided a savings account with no February activity shows no_data with null balances — honest but noisy — and filed a follow-up. The import work landed the answer first: a quiet month inside verified evidence is complete, with the last stated balance carried into both opening and closing.

So the example table in the comment is wrong now. Module 05 on this page shows the current behaviour. This is the single biggest divergence and it is an improvement.

booking_date is now transaction_datealready done

You confirmed that Danske's Dato is the purchase date, not the booking date. The comment's field list still says booking_date; the PR has been rebased onto the rename. Nothing to do.

Missing balances are now nearly unreachableQ18 narrowed

The import rules quarantine a balance-stating export that has a missing balance, so missing_balance now only arises for a source that states no balances at all. Danske isn't one. The rule is still correct; the case it covers is hypothetical for you today.

The account gained two fields the comment never mentions

coverage_start and evidence_through on GoldAccount. They are how an account with no transactions at all can still be reported as no_data rather than silently vanishing from the dashboard.

Two gaps to close before this merges

gold-contract.mdevidence_through is written the old waycheck

PR #18 defines it as "day before the latest admitted export date". That is exactly the definition the prototype broke — download all of 2025 today and the system concludes its evidence reaches yesterday, so January to March read complete with zero activity. PR #31 fixes it by declaring separately how far an export reaches.

Gold's coverage is computed from this field. If #18 merges on top of #31 without adopting the new wording, the fixed bug comes back at the Gold layer. The wording #31 lands is already in the repo; this line needs to match it.

gold-contract.mdThe changelog is written against the wrong versioncheck

The table is headed "Changes from 0.1", but the branch it merges into already carries a different 0.2 — the one that added balances and account listing. Three members of that version disappear without appearing in the table:

  • boundary_transactions() — no longer needed, because Gold computes coverage itself now.
  • category_direction on the transaction — moved onto the category dimension, where it belongs.
  • day_sequence on the transaction — replaced by account_sequence.

Every one of those removals is justified by a decision on this page. None of them is written down as a change, so anyone reading the changelog to migrate will miss them. ADR-006 also still names boundary_transactions() and the old field names; ADR-007 says it supersedes ADR-006 on exactly these points, so the text is stale rather than wrong — but it reads as wrong.

The thing to remember

The expensive decision here is not the star schema or the lineage split — both of those are reversible refactors on a project with no code yet. The expensive one is the grain. Everything you classify, correct and annotate from now on hangs off "one row = one booked transaction", and changing that later means every one of those hangs off nothing.

That is why ADR-008 exists as its own document for a decision that, read quickly, looks like it decided to do less work.
